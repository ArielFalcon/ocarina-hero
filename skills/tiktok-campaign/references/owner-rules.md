# Rules for every film

General principles from the owner's reviews. Add a new rule only when it applies beyond one film, written as a principle.

## Songs

- Every song is instantly recognisable to a general audience, not only the opener.
- Freshness: avoid songs used often in earlier films, above all as openers. Check the opener ledger and the recent films before choosing.
- The largest text while a song plays is the name people know it by, never a technical or soundtrack title. A pack headline names the song ("Learn the X song") with the game as subtitle.
- Each song plays long enough to recognise it, in the part where its tune is identifiable, and ends at the end of a phrase.
- The buttons of the song being played are on screen whenever it plays, and nothing covers them.
- Melodies and buttons are captured from the live product, never redrawn or synthesised from memory.
- When a film quotes a famous sound or meme, the music continues the way the meme does.

## Text on screen

- Numbers that change (the song count) are never exact: use a rounded, punchy call ("Play 300+ songs").
- The site is presented as a free website, not an app, visible the whole film and large in the last frame, inside the platform's safe zone.
- Progress indicators stay small; headlines carry information, not labels.
- Plain words; no em or en dashes.

## Story and staging

- The hook is on frame 0; nothing delays or covers it.
- Characters keep their canonical companions and details, and props stay coherent from shot to shot.
- Props obey the story's logic; a gag that contradicts its own premise breaks the joke.
- Every beat holds long enough to be read; songs chosen for a story relate to its plot.
- Keep the established visual style of earlier films; avoid devices the owner has called clichés.
